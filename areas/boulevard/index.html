Boulevard, Athens, GA

The Boulevard Historic District is roughly bounded by the railroad tracks, Pulaski Street, Prince Avenue, and Hiawassee Street. It was founded in the late 1800s and grew as a streetcar suburb. It is named for its widest street and is listed on the National Register of Historic Places.

Local guides describe Neoclassical and Queen Anne homes along Boulevard and brightly painted wood cottages on the side streets, many once home to textile mill workers. The area is mostly residential.

Cottages, larger homes, and nearby Pulaski Heights

Next to Boulevard are Pulaski Heights and the North Chase area, less than a mile from downtown. Guides describe renovated cottages and modern homes on hilly streets, plus lofts in a former industrial area near North Chase Street.

That mix means different jobs. A small cottage may have a tight utility space where a water heater has to fit. A loft conversion may have newer plumbing in an older building. We look at what is there and plan the work around it.

What we help with

Common calls include a leaking faucet, a toilet that runs, a slow drain, low pressure at one fixture, and a water heater that has stopped heating. Tell dispatch what you see and we will explain what we would check first.

If you suspect a hidden leak, our leak detection work locates it before anything is opened up. If water is spreading, close the main water valve and call us right away.

Hilly streets and drainage around the house

On a sloped lot, water around the house can come from the ground rather than a pipe. If you see water in a crawl space or basement after rain, that is not always a plumbing leak. We tell you what we find and say so if the cause looks like drainage.

If a sump pump or drain is part of the answer, we can install or repair it. Our sump pump page explains how we approach it, and our drain cleaning page covers clogged lines.

Questions about plumbing in Boulevard

Do you work on cottages and older homes in Boulevard?

Do you cover Pulaski Heights and North Chase too?

Is water in my crawl space always a plumbing leak?

No. Water can come from a pipe or from the ground after rain. We check for a pipe leak first and tell you if it looks like a drainage problem instead.

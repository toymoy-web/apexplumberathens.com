Emergency water line repair: no water or water in the yard

A water line problem is an emergency when you have no water at the house, when water is bubbling up in the yard, or when a spot near the meter box stays wet. The line that runs from the meter to the house is buried, so the first sign is often the ground, not the plumbing.

Athens-Clarke Public Utilities lists a Water and Sewer line at 706-613-3495 on weekdays and an after-hours line at 706-613-3481. Call them if the problem may be on the public side of the meter, and call us for the line on your side or if you are not sure where the leak is.

Call right away if

  • No water at any tap in the house
  • Water bubbling up in the yard or along the line to the house
  • A wet or sunken spot near the meter box or the cleanout
  • Low water pressure that appeared suddenly in every fixture

"The meter's running and I don't know why"

Calls about this usually start with a suspicion that the leak is somewhere between the meter and the house, based on the water bill or a visibly soggy yard, rather than a request for water line repair by name. That's exactly the right instinct: the water service line, the pipe connecting the meter to the home, is a common and often overlooked leak location.

Athens' shallow bedrock changes how this job runs

Granite bedrock runs shallow in parts of Athens and unincorporated Clarke County, which is a real, practical complication for water line work: a straightforward trench dig elsewhere can mean rock-cutting equipment here. We quote water line repair and replacement with that possibility factored in upfront, rather than surprising a homeowner with it mid-job once we hit rock.

Properties along Athens' older platted streets sometimes have a water service line that predates current mapping records entirely. That is part of why we always confirm the line's actual path with detection equipment rather than relying on a decades-old utility sketch that may not reflect what's really in the ground.

Mature trees and old service lines

Cobbham's houses sit on tree-lined streets, and the water service line from the meter to an older house often runs under roots, sidewalks, and landscaping. That is where trenchless replacement earns its place, because it avoids opening the whole yard. We locate the line before we recommend a method.

Signs to watch for

Signs you need water line repair & replacement

  • A soggy or unusually green patch of yard between the meter and the house
  • Water meter running with every fixture in the house off
  • Low pressure at every fixture in the house, not just one
  • An unexplained spike in the water bill

How we diagnose and fix it

We confirm the leak location with leak detection equipment first. Then we decide between a trenchless method, which pulls a new line through the existing path without a full open trench, and traditional excavation. The yard's layout, existing landscaping, and whether bedrock is a factor all go into that choice.

A single, isolated failure point on an otherwise sound line is usually a spot repair. A line that's failed more than once, or is old enough to be broadly unreliable, is more honestly addressed with a full replacement, which we can often do trenchlessly to avoid tearing up the whole yard.

Water service line replacement requires a permit under Georgia's plumbing code (the International Plumbing Code, or IPC, with state amendments), and the new line's material and burial depth have to meet code minimums for freeze protection and durability. We handle that as standard.

Avoiding the next call

Because Athens' shallow bedrock can turn a routine water line repair into a more involved job, it's worth confirming the line's condition proactively on an older home rather than discovering the bedrock issue mid-emergency when the line has already failed. We'll also confirm the exact meter-to-house distance before quoting, since a longer run changes both the material cost and the labor time meaningfully.

Meter to house

Leaks between the meter and the house

A buried line can leak for weeks before it shows. Clues include a patch of unusually green grass over the line, a wet area near the meter box, a meter that moves with everything off, and a bill that keeps climbing. Callers sometimes see all of these together.

We locate the leak along the line first. Then we explain whether a spot repair makes sense or whether a longer section should be replaced. Which part of the line belongs to you and which part belongs to the utility is something to confirm with the utility, and we can tell you where the leak sits so you know what to ask.

Wet spot or soft ground

Standing water in a dry spell, or ground that stays soft along the line, is a strong clue.

Meter moves with everything off

Close the main valve at the house. If the meter then stops, the loss is on the house side of the meter.

Old line to replace

Some homeowners ask for the whole line to be replaced instead of patched. We quote that separately from a spot repair.

Low pressure and no water

Low pressure, no water, and older lines

Low pressure in every fixture at once points to the line, the main valve, or the pressure regulator, not a single faucet. No water at all can be a shutoff, a failed line, or a utility outage. Check with neighbors, then call.

If you suspect an older service line, such as lead or galvanized steel, tell dispatch. We can look at the line where it enters the house and explain replacement options after we see it.

FAQ

Emergency questions about water line repair & replacement in Athens

How do I know if my water line, not my house plumbing, is leaking?

A soggy area in the yard between the meter and the house, or a meter that keeps running with all fixtures off, both point to the service line specifically. We confirm it with leak detection.

Will you have to dig up my whole yard?

Not necessarily. Trenchless replacement pulls a new line through the existing path in many cases, avoiding a full open trench.

Does Athens' rocky ground make this more expensive?

It can, if bedrock is shallow along the line's path. We check for that upfront and quote it honestly rather than as a surprise mid-job.

What should I do if I have no water at all?

Check whether neighbors have water and whether the main valve at the house is fully open. If water is missing only at your house, or you see water in the yard, call dispatch. We can locate the problem and repair the line.

Who do I call if the leak is at the meter box?

Athens-Clarke Public Utilities lists 706-613-3495 on weekdays and 706-613-3481 after hours. If you are not sure whether the leak is public or private, call us as well and we can say where it sits.

How do you find a leak in a buried water line?

We use acoustic and thermal equipment and meter checks to narrow the location, then confirm before digging. You get a plain explanation of the repair before work begins.

Can you replace the whole line from the meter to the house?

Yes. We explain whether a spot repair or a longer replacement is the better answer, and we quote the replacement separately so you can compare.

I think I may have lead pipe. Can you check?

Tell dispatch when you call. We can look at the line where it enters the house and discuss replacement options once we see it.

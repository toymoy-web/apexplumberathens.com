Water Line Repair & Replacement in Athens, GA

Low pressure at every fixture, an unexplained spike in the water bill, or a soggy patch in the yard between the meter and the house points to a main water line problem, not something inside the house at all.

"The meter's running and I don't know why"

Calls about this usually start with a suspicion that the leak is somewhere between the meter and the house, based on the water bill or a visibly soggy yard, rather than a request for water line repair by name. That's exactly the right instinct: the water service line, the pipe connecting the meter to the home, is a common and often overlooked leak location.

Athens' shallow bedrock changes how this job runs

Granite bedrock runs shallow in parts of Athens and unincorporated Clarke County, which is a real, practical complication for water line work: a straightforward trench dig elsewhere can mean rock-cutting equipment here. We quote water line repair and replacement with that possibility factored in upfront, rather than surprising a homeowner with it mid-job once we hit rock.

Properties along Athens' older platted streets sometimes have a water service line that predates current mapping records entirely, which is part of why we always confirm the line's actual path with detection equipment rather than relying on a decades-old utility sketch that may not reflect what's really in the ground.

Signs to watch for

Signs you need water line repair & replacement

  • A soggy or unusually green patch of yard between the meter and the house
  • Water meter running with every fixture in the house off
  • Low pressure at every fixture in the house, not just one
  • An unexplained spike in the water bill

How we diagnose and fix it

We confirm the leak location with leak detection equipment first, then determine whether a trenchless method (pulling a new line through the existing path without a full open trench) or traditional excavation is the right fit, based on the yard's layout, existing landscaping, and whether bedrock is a factor.

A single, isolated failure point on an otherwise sound line is usually a spot repair. A line that's failed more than once, or is old enough to be broadly unreliable, is more honestly addressed with a full replacement, which we can often do trenchlessly to avoid tearing up the whole yard.

Water service line replacement requires a permit under Georgia's plumbing code (the International Plumbing Code, or IPC, with state amendments), and the new line's material and burial depth have to meet code minimums for freeze protection and durability; we handle that as standard.

Avoiding the next call

Because Athens' shallow bedrock can turn a routine water line repair into a more involved job, it's worth confirming the line's condition proactively on an older home rather than discovering the bedrock issue mid-emergency when the line has already failed. We'll also confirm the exact meter-to-house distance before quoting, since a longer run changes both the material cost and the labor time meaningfully.

FAQ

Questions about water line repair & replacement in Athens

How do I know if my water line, not my house plumbing, is leaking?

A soggy area in the yard between the meter and the house, or a meter that keeps running with all fixtures off, both point to the service line specifically. We confirm it with leak detection.

Will you have to dig up my whole yard?

Not necessarily. Trenchless replacement pulls a new line through the existing path in many cases, avoiding a full open trench.

Does Athens' rocky ground make this more expensive?

It can, if bedrock is shallow along the line's path. We check for that upfront and quote it honestly rather than as a surprise mid-job.

Repiping & Whole-Home Pipe Replacement in Athens, GA

When a house has had more than one pipe failure, or the plumbing is original galvanized or early copper from a home built decades ago, repiping addresses the whole system instead of chasing one leak after another.

Multiple leaks, low pressure everywhere, discolored water

This usually comes up after a second or third leak call, or when a homeowner notices low water pressure and discolored water across multiple fixtures, not because someone searched for repiping by name. Those symptoms together point to a system-wide pipe age problem, and whole-home repiping is the honest long-term fix rather than another one-off patch.

Why this comes up often in Athens specifically

A significant share of Athens' housing stock, especially in the older in-town neighborhoods and much of the University of Georgia rental market, still runs on galvanized steel or early copper supply lines original to homes built well before modern plumbing standards. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out, which is the direct, practical reason a house can have declining water pressure and rust-colored water for years before an outright failure forces the issue.

A house that's changed hands a few times in Athens' resale market sometimes comes with partial, piecemeal plumbing updates from different owners rather than one coherent system, which can mean a mix of galvanized, copper, and even older polybutylene sections. A whole-home repipe is often the point where all of that gets brought up to one consistent standard.

Signs to watch for

Signs you need repiping & whole-home pipe replacement

  • Rust-colored water, especially after the tap has sat unused
  • Noticeably declining water pressure over months or years
  • More than one pipe leak in the past year
  • Visible corrosion or pitting on exposed pipe (basement, crawlspace)
Before we arrive

What to do right now

  • Know where your main water shutoff is before work begins, so the household isn't caught without water unexpectedly
  • Clear access to work areas (under sinks, in closets, in the attic) ahead of the crew's arrival
  • Ask which sections of the home will lose water service on which day, and for how long
  • Document existing wall and ceiling condition with photos before cutting begins, for your own records

How we diagnose and fix it

We assess the existing system's material and condition, plan a repiping route that minimizes wall and ceiling disruption where possible, and replace supply lines with modern PEX or copper, bringing the whole system up to current code in the process.

A full whole-home repipe is the right call when galvanized pipe is confirmed throughout, or after repeated leaks in different locations. A partial repipe, addressing just the worst section, can make sense if the rest of the system is confirmed sound. We'll be honest about which situation actually applies.

Georgia's plumbing code (the International Plumbing Code, or IPC, with state amendments) specifies approved materials (PEX, copper, and others) for potable water repiping and requires permit and inspection for whole-home replacement; we pull permits and schedule inspections as part of the job, not as a separate hassle for the homeowner.

Avoiding the next call

A home with confirmed galvanized supply lines is worth repiping proactively once pressure starts declining, rather than waiting for a failure, since galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside in a way that's invisible until it isn't. We can assess pipe material and condition during any other service call, at no extra charge, for households wondering where they stand.

  • Existing pipe material (galvanized is typically the most involved to replace)
  • Wall and ceiling access, and drywall repair afterward

FAQ

Questions about repiping & whole-home pipe replacement in Athens

How do I know if my house has galvanized pipe?

Discolored water, especially after the water's been sitting, declining pressure over time, and a home built before the 1960s to 1970s are strong signs. We can confirm it during an inspection.

Do I have to move out during a repipe?

Not usually. We work in sections to keep at least some water service available, and most repipes are completed within a few days.

Will repiping fix low water pressure?

If the low pressure is caused by corroded galvanized pipe restricting flow, yes, repiping resolves it directly.

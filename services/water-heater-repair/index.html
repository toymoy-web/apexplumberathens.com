Water Heater Repair in Athens, GA

No hot water, a puddle at the base of the tank, or a rotten-egg smell from the faucet: these are the calls we get most for water heater repair in Athens, and most of them are fixable same-day.

What actually fails first on an Athens water heater

The anode rod and the lower heating element take the brunt of Athens' moderately mineral-heavy water. Sediment settles at the bottom of the tank faster here than in a soft-water city, which insulates the lower element and makes it work harder, and that's the direct, practical reason a tank water heater in an older in-town Athens home often needs its first repair sooner than the manufacturer's typical timeline suggests. In University of Georgia rental housing especially, where the unit is original to the house, we see anode rods fully consumed well ahead of schedule.

In University of Georgia rental housing specifically, where multiple tenants share a single water heater sized for a smaller household, we see repeat repair calls tied to a unit that's simply undersized for its actual demand, not failing from age. That's worth flagging to a landlord even when the immediate call is a straightforward repair.

Signs to watch for

Signs you need water heater repair

  • No hot water, or it runs out much faster than before
  • Water heater making popping or rumbling sounds (usually sediment)
  • Water has a metallic or rotten-egg smell
  • Visible moisture or rust at the base of the tank
Before we arrive

What to do right now

  • Do not attempt to relight a gas pilot yourself if you smell gas near the unit, leave the area and call your gas utility first
  • Turn off power at the breaker before inspecting an electric unit for a suspected leak
  • Avoid touching the tank or pipes near a suspected leak until power or gas is confirmed off
  • Call promptly if you notice a rotten-egg smell, that can indicate a gas safety issue, not just a water heater issue

How we diagnose and fix it

We test the thermostat and heating elements directly with a multimeter rather than guessing from symptoms alone, check the anode rod condition, flush accumulated sediment, and pressure-test the tank if a leak is suspected. A leaking tank gets identified as a tank issue, not confused with a supply-line or fitting leak nearby.

A tank under 8 years old with a failed element or thermostat is almost always a repair, not a replacement. Past 10 years, or with a confirmed tank leak, we'll give an honest repair-versus-replace comparison rather than pushing a replacement on a unit that still has useful life left.

Tank water heaters are required under Georgia's plumbing code (the International Plumbing Code, or IPC, with state amendments) to have a properly sized temperature-and-pressure relief valve with a compliant discharge line; we check this on every repair visit even when it isn't the reason for the call, since it's a real safety item, not an upsell.

Avoiding the next call

An annual sediment flush and an anode rod check add years to a tank water heater's service life in Athens specifically, given how much faster sediment accumulates here than in soft-water regions. We can add this to a service visit even when the original call was for something else, since it's a cheap way to avoid the next emergency call.

FAQ

Questions about water heater repair in Athens

Why does my water heater smell like rotten eggs?

That's usually a reaction between the anode rod and bacteria in the water, not a gas leak. We can replace the rod and flush the tank to resolve it.

Is a leaking water heater an emergency?

Yes, call right away. A tank leak only gets worse, and it can damage flooring and drywall the longer it runs.

Do you repair both gas and electric water heaters?

Yes, we repair both, along with tankless systems.

How much does water heater repair usually cost?

It depends on the specific part, an element or thermostat repair is on the lower end, a full tank replacement is more; we give a real number before starting, not an estimate range.

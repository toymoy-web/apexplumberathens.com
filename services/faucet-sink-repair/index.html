Faucet & Sink Repair in Athens, GA

A dripping faucet, low flow at a single fixture, or a leak under the sink: faucet and sink repair covers the fixtures and connections that don't need a full plumber-scale job but still need doing right.

The role Athens' mineral-heavy water plays

Athens' moderately mineral-heavy water leaves scale buildup inside faucet cartridges and aerators over time, which is the direct, practical reason a faucet that's dripped for months is often a worn cartridge coated in scale rather than a simple washer replacement. That same buildup is why aerators clog and flow drops at individual fixtures even when the rest of the house has normal pressure.

Kitchen faucets in Athens see the heaviest mineral exposure of any fixture in the house, simply from frequency of use, which is why kitchen faucet cartridges tend to fail before bathroom ones in the same home. If one fixture is dripping, it's worth a quick check of the others while we're already there.

Signs to watch for

Signs you need faucet & sink repair

  • A faucet that drips even when fully closed
  • Reduced water flow at one specific fixture
  • Water pooling under the sink cabinet
  • A handle that's become loose or hard to turn
  • Visible corrosion or mineral crust building up around a fixture's base

How we diagnose and fix it

We identify whether the issue is the cartridge, the valve seat, a worn O-ring, or scale-clogged aerator, and replace or clean the specific part rather than swapping the whole fixture unnecessarily.

Most drips and low-flow issues resolve with a cartridge replacement or aerator cleaning. A fixture old enough that replacement parts aren't available, or with a cracked body, is when full faucet replacement makes more sense.

Avoiding the next call

Cleaning aerators every few months is a simple, free habit that offsets Athens' mineral-heavy water and keeps flow strong at every fixture without needing a repair call, especially in kitchens where the aerator sees constant use. We'll also check the shutoff valves under the sink while we're there, since a stuck or corroded valve is a small thing to fix now versus a problem during a future repair. A fixture that's original to a home built decades ago is sometimes worth replacing outright rather than repairing repeatedly, and we'll say so plainly rather than billing for a third repair on the same worn-out faucet. We'll also look at the supply line and shutoff valve while we're under the sink, since a stiff or corroded valve is worth flagging even when it isn't the reason for today's call. A single service visit can usually address more than one fixture at a time, which is worth mentioning if you've been putting off a second, smaller drip elsewhere in the house. Kitchen and bathroom fixtures wear at different rates depending on use, so a whole-house fixture check every couple of years catches small issues before they become bigger repairs.

FAQ

Questions about faucet & sink repair in Athens

Why does my faucet drip even after I tightened it?

A drip is almost always a worn internal cartridge or valve seat, not something a handle tightening fixes. Scale buildup from Athens' water often accelerates that wear.

Can you match my existing faucet brand for a repair?

In most cases yes. For older or discontinued models where parts aren't available, we'll recommend replacement instead.

Why is water flow weak at just one faucet?

Usually a clogged aerator from mineral buildup, an easy fix, or occasionally a partially closed shutoff valve.

Can a single dripping faucet actually raise my water bill?

Yes, a steady drip can waste thousands of gallons a year. It's a small repair that pays for itself in most households within a few months.

Sewer Line Repair in Athens, GA

A sewer backing up into the house, slow drains across every fixture at once, or a sewer smell in the yard usually points to a main line problem, not a single clogged drain.

Tree roots: the recurring cause in Athens

Athens' tree-lined older neighborhoods, a real and visible feature of the city's landscape, are also the leading practical reason sewer lines fail here. Root systems from mature trees seek out the moisture inside a sewer line and infiltrate through small cracks or joints, especially in the clay or older cast-iron pipe common in Athens' pre-1980s housing stock. A line that's been slowly narrowing from root intrusion for years often doesn't show symptoms until it backs up all at once.

Athens' older cast-iron sewer lines, common in pre-1960s construction downtown and in nearby historic neighborhoods, corrode from the inside in a way that's distinct from clay-pipe root intrusion. The two need different repair approaches even though both show up as a backup.

Signs to watch for

Signs you need sewer line repair

  • Multiple drains backing up at the same time
  • Gurgling sounds from drains when other fixtures are used
  • Sewage smell in the yard, especially near mature trees
  • A drain that clears with snaking but returns within weeks

How we diagnose and fix it

We run a camera inspection first to see exactly where the line is compromised, whether it's root intrusion, a cracked joint, or a bellied (sagging) section, before recommending a fix. For root intrusion without structural damage, hydro jetting or mechanical cutting clears the line. For a cracked or collapsed section, spot repair or trenchless lining addresses the actual damage.

Not every root-intrusion call needs full line replacement. If the camera inspection shows an otherwise sound line with a localized root entry point, a spot repair or trenchless lining is usually the honest, lower-cost fix. Full replacement is reserved for lines with widespread damage across their length.

Repairs that involve excavating or replacing a section of sewer line typically require a permit under Georgia's plumbing code (the International Plumbing Code, or IPC, with state amendments), and the replacement material and slope have to meet code minimums; we handle that as a standard part of any sewer line repair.

Avoiding the next call

In neighborhoods with large, established trees, which describes a lot of Athens' most desirable older streets, a camera inspection every few years on a home's sewer line is worth the cost simply to catch root intrusion before it causes a full backup. Waiting for the symptom means waiting for the worse, more expensive version of the same problem.

FAQ

Questions about sewer line repair in Athens

How do I know if my sewer problem is roots or something else?

A camera inspection shows this directly. Root intrusion has a distinctive appearance on camera compared to a crack, sag, or grease buildup.

Can you fix a sewer line without digging up my yard?

Often yes. Trenchless pipe lining repairs a damaged section from the inside for many root-intrusion and crack cases, without excavating the whole line.

Do you also do sewer camera inspections on their own?

Yes, a standalone camera inspection is available if you just need to confirm the line's condition before deciding on a repair.

How often should I get my sewer line inspected?

Every 2 to 3 years for a home with mature trees nearby is a reasonable preventive schedule; more often if there's a known history of root intrusion.

Can I see the camera inspection footage?

Yes, we're glad to walk you through what the camera found before recommending a repair, so you understand exactly what you're paying for.

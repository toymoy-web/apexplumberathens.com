Signs to watch for

Signs you need commercial plumbing

  • Grease trap due for scheduled cleaning or showing signs of overflow
  • Backflow assembly due for annual testing or certification
  • Multiple units or tenants reporting the same drainage issue
  • New buildout or renovation needing code-compliant rough-in
  • A new tenant build-out that needs code-compliant fixture and drain rough-in

How we diagnose and fix it

For grease trap cleaning, we pump and clean the trap and document the service for health-code inspection records. For backflow testing and prevention, we test the assembly against Georgia's commercial backflow requirements and handle certification paperwork where the property needs it on file.

We'll size any commercial fixture, water heater, or drain line work to the property's real usage, not a generic residential spec, and coordinate scheduling around business hours where downtime matters.

Georgia's plumbing code (the International Plumbing Code, or IPC, with state amendments) requires backflow prevention assemblies on qualifying commercial connections to be tested annually by a certified tester, with documentation kept on file. We provide that certification as part of the service. We'll also confirm whether a specific fixture count triggers additional code requirements before work begins.

Avoiding the next call

A scheduled grease trap cleaning and backflow testing calendar, set up once, keeps a restaurant or commercial property ahead of health-code inspections instead of scrambling before one. We can set that schedule up as part of an initial service visit so it isn't something the property manager has to track manually.

For multi-tenant buildings, we can also coordinate scheduling with the property manager directly, rather than requiring each tenant to call separately for a shared line issue. Restaurants renewing a lease or opening a second location in Athens can also ask us to review the space's existing plumbing before signing, to flag any code work needed ahead of opening day.

We'll also review whether a property's current fixture count and drain capacity still match its actual use, since a space that's changed tenants or purpose over the years sometimes outgrows its original plumbing without anyone noticing until a problem shows up.

Floor drains and grease

Floor drains, grates, and grease traps

Emergency slab leak repair: signs you should not wait on

A slab leak is an emergency when water is coming up through the floor or the meter is spinning fast with everything off. Water under floorboards, a warm patch on the floor, or the sound of running water inside a quiet house are the usual signs.

Close the main valve and call. If the leak is on a line that feeds a single fixture, tell dispatch which fixture, since that sometimes lets us isolate it and restore water to the rest of the house while we plan the repair.

Call right away if

  • Water seeping up through a floor, baseboard, or garage slab
  • A warm or hot spot on a floor with no heating in that area
  • Sound of running water with everything off and the meter moving
  • Water coming up through a driveway or patio slab

Warm spots, rising water bills, and no visible source

Slab leak isn't the term most people use when they call. It's usually described as a warm patch on the floor, water at the edge of the concrete near the garage or foundation, or a bill that's climbed without any change in usage. Those are the exact symptoms of a slab leak, a pressurized water line running under the concrete foundation that has cracked or corroded.

Why slab leaks are common in Athens specifically

Pipe in or under a concrete slab can fail from age, corrosion, or a poor fitting, and ground movement is one possible factor we check. Homes built on slab foundations from the 1960s through the 1980s, common in several of Athens' in-town neighborhoods, are the ones we see most often, since the copper supply lines from that era are also now old enough to be more vulnerable to that stress.

This is why we locate the leak first and look at pipe age and material before we say what caused it.

Midcentury slabs in Normaltown

Normaltown has plenty of midcentury homes, and houses from that era are more likely to sit on a concrete slab than the 1800s homes in nearby Cobbham. The copper under those slabs has had decades of service. If your home was built in that window, an unexplained water bill is worth a leak detection visit first.

Signs to watch for

Signs you need slab leak repair

  • A warm patch on the floor with no heating element nearby
  • The sound of running water when every fixture is off
  • Cracks appearing in flooring or baseboards near the slab edge
  • Mold or mildew smell rising from the floor
Before we arrive

Emergency steps: what to do right now

  • Shut off the main water valve if the leak location isn't confirmed yet and it's safe to do so
  • Avoid running additional water into the affected area while waiting for diagnosis
  • Photograph any visible warping, cracking, or moisture for insurance purposes
  • Don't attempt to cut into the slab yourself before the leak is precisely located

How we diagnose and fix it

We locate the leak precisely with acoustic and thermal equipment before recommending a repair path, since guessing at a slab location means unnecessary concrete cutting. Once confirmed, the repair is either a direct spot repair through the slab or a reroute that runs a new line through the attic or an accessible wall cavity, bypassing the slab entirely.

A single, isolated slab leak in an otherwise sound copper system usually makes sense as a spot repair. If the same house has had more than one slab leak, or the copper is showing broader pitting, a full reroute is often the more honest long-term recommendation, even though it costs more upfront, because it stops the next leak before it happens.

A slab leak repair that exposes plumbing under a permitted structure typically requires a permit and inspection under Georgia's plumbing code (the International Plumbing Code, or IPC, with state amendments) before the concrete is closed back up. We handle that paperwork as part of the job.

Avoiding the next call

Homes built directly on a slab in Athens' older neighborhoods are the ones we'd flag for a proactive leak-detection check every few years, because the pipe under the slab is old and cannot be seen. Catching a slab leak from a water-bill anomaly is always cheaper than catching it from visible flooring damage.

Slabs, driveways and garages

Leaks under floors, garages, and concrete drives

Most slab leaks start where a supply or drain line runs under concrete. The edge of a garage slab that meets the foundation, the area under a kitchen or bathroom, and the line to a water heater are common starting points. Water can also surface in a driveway or a patio when the leak is outside the house footprint.

We locate the leak first, so the cut in the concrete is small and in the right place. When access through concrete is the only option we explain how big the opening will be, and we talk through the repair path before we start.

Crawlspaces and raised foundations

Under-house leaks on crawlspace homes

Many Athens homes sit above a crawlspace instead of a slab. A leak under the house is easier to reach, but it can run for a long time before anyone sees it. Look for a damp floor, a musty smell, or a floor that feels spongy near a bathroom or kitchen.

If the crawlspace is dry and the meter still moves, the loss may be in a buried line. We check both before we recommend a repair, and when a line under the house has failed more than once, rerouting it overhead can be worth discussing.

FAQ

Emergency questions about slab leak repair in Athens

How do I know if I have a slab leak versus a regular leak?

A warm spot on the floor with no plumbing fixture nearby, water at the edge of the foundation, or an unexplained rise in the water bill are the strongest signs. Leak detection confirms it precisely before any repair starts.

Do you have to cut through my whole floor to fix a slab leak?

No. Once the leak is located, the repair is typically a small, targeted cut, or in some cases a reroute that avoids the slab entirely.

Is a slab leak covered by homeowner's insurance?

Often the water damage portion is, though policies vary. We recommend photographing the damage and checking with your insurer. We can provide documentation of the plumbing cause.

Can a slab leak get worse if I wait?

Yes, it can undermine the slab over time and lead to more extensive structural and flooring damage the longer it runs, beyond just the water bill.

Is a slab leak an emergency?

It can be. Water coming up through a floor or a meter that spins fast with everything off should be treated as an emergency. Close the main valve and call dispatch. A slow loss on a line you can isolate is urgent but may leave time to schedule.

Can you find the exact spot before cutting concrete?

Yes. We use acoustic and thermal equipment to locate the leak before any cutting, so the opening is small and in the right place. We explain what the repair will involve before we start.

Can you cut into a concrete driveway or garage slab?

Yes, when that is the right access to the line. We talk through the size of the opening and the repair options first, since sometimes rerouting the line is a better answer than cutting.

How do I know the leak is under the slab and not in a wall?

A warm spot on a floor, water appearing at the edge of a slab, or a meter that moves with every fixture off point to a slab leak. A wet wall or ceiling suggests a different path. Tell dispatch what you see.

I had to shut the water off at the meter. What now?

Call dispatch and tell us you closed the meter. We plan the visit around the leak, and we can talk about restoring water to the rest of the house once the leaking line is isolated.

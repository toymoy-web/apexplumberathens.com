Sizing it right for Athens households

A big share of Athens housing is either older in-town homes with original, undersized tanks or student rental units where multiple bathrooms run on a single small heater. Both of those are a genuine practical sizing problem, not just an age problem: the fix isn't always a like-for-like replacement, it's often correcting the tank size or switching format entirely so simultaneous showers stop draining the hot water supply. On the commercial side, downtown Athens restaurants and UGA-area businesses have their own capacity requirements we size separately.

A lot of Athens' rental housing stock has water heaters tucked into small closets or attics with limited clearance, which affects which replacement units are even physically viable before cost becomes the deciding factor. We check clearance and access as part of any replacement estimate, not after the unit's already been ordered.

Signs to watch for

Signs you need water heater installation & replacement

  • Current unit is leaking or over 10 years old
  • Household regularly runs out of hot water with multiple people showering
  • Considering a switch to tankless or heat-pump format
  • Rust-colored water coming specifically from the hot tap
Before we arrive

What to do right now

  • Clear access to the current unit's location before the crew arrives
  • Know where your main water shutoff and, if applicable, gas shutoff are located
  • Plan for a period without hot water during the swap, we'll give you a real time window
  • Ask about drain pan and expansion tank requirements if your current setup doesn't have them

How we diagnose and fix it

We calculate actual peak hot water demand for the household or business, size the replacement accordingly, and handle permitting, gas or electrical hookup, venting, and code-compliant drain-pan and expansion-tank requirements as part of the install, not as an add-on discovered mid-job.

Tank, tankless, and heat-pump water heaters are all real options depending on the household's usage pattern, available electrical or gas capacity, and space. We'll walk through the honest tradeoffs of each rather than defaulting to whatever was already installed.

Georgia's plumbing code (the International Plumbing Code, or IPC, with state amendments) requires a code-compliant expansion tank on a closed system and a properly terminated temperature-and-pressure relief line on every installation; we include both as standard, not as an add-on discovered after the fact.

Avoiding the next call

Getting the sizing right at installation is what prevents the most common post-install complaint we hear elsewhere, which is a new unit that still runs out of hot water because it was sized to the old unit's capacity instead of the household's actual peak demand. We calculate that demand directly rather than assuming a like-for-like swap is correct.

FAQ

Questions about water heater installation & replacement in Athens

How long does a water heater installation take?

Most standard tank replacements take half a day. A switch to tankless or heat-pump format, or any electrical/venting upgrade, can take longer; we'll give a real estimate before starting.

Should I switch to tankless when I replace my tank?

It depends on the household's hot water usage and available gas or electrical capacity. We'll go through the real tradeoffs, including Athens' mineral-heavy water and the descaling that comes with a tankless unit, before recommending either way.

Do you handle water heater installation for commercial properties?

Yes, including higher-capacity units for restaurants and other commercial spaces in and around downtown Athens.

Do you remove and dispose of my old water heater?

Yes, removal and disposal of the old unit is included as part of the installation.

Do you offer financing for water heater replacement?

Ask us directly about current options; we'll always give you the straight cost first so you can decide what works best.

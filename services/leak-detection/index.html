Emergency leak detection: when a hidden leak cannot wait

A hidden leak becomes an emergency when water is visibly spreading or the meter keeps running with every fixture off. A stain growing on a ceiling, water seeping up through a floor, or a pool in the yard that was dry yesterday all fit. Call dispatch at any hour and describe where you see the water.

If you can reach the main valve, close it and check whether the meter stops. That one test tells us whether the loss is inside the house or between the meter and the house, and it helps us bring the right equipment on the first visit.

Call right away if

  • The water meter is spinning with every fixture and appliance off
  • A ceiling, wall, or floor is wet or staining and you cannot see where it starts
  • Water is surfacing in the yard or by the meter box
  • A utility alert or a sudden bill spike says you are losing water

"I don't know where it's coming from"

Most leak detection calls start exactly that way: something's wrong, the water bill or the meter proves it, but there's no visible source. That's the normal starting point, not a hard case. Leak detection is the diagnostic step that finds the actual location electronically, using acoustic and thermal equipment, so a repair crew isn't guessing at where to open a wall or dig a yard.

Why Athens leaks are often harder to see than elsewhere

Athens sits in the Georgia Piedmont, where red clay overlies granite. Ground moisture and movement are one of several things we consider, along with pipe age, material, and pressure, because a small crack can go unseen above ground for a long time. In homes built directly on a concrete slab, that shift is the leading practical reason a leak shows up as a water-bill spike weeks before it ever shows up as a wet spot on the floor.

Homes with a documented history of foundation or slab issues, common in some of Athens' 1960s-to-1980s in-town neighborhoods, are worth a proactive leak-detection sweep every few years. The same soil movement that caused a problem once tends to keep putting slow, small stresses on the plumbing over time.

Hills change water pressure around Athens

Streets like Finley, which climbs steeply to the Tree That Owns Itself at Dearing Street, show how hilly in-town Athens is. Water pressure is higher at the bottom of a hill than at the top, so a small leak can show up differently on two houses a few blocks apart. We check pressure at the house before we start listening for the leak.

Signs to watch for

Signs you need leak detection

  • Water bill increased with no change in usage
  • Water meter still spins with every fixture in the house off
  • A damp or warm spot on flooring with no obvious source
  • A musty smell in one area of the house without visible water
Before we arrive

Emergency steps: what to do right now

  • Note when your water bill first changed, that timeline helps narrow down the cause
  • Check your water meter with everything off, if it's still spinning, that confirms a leak somewhere in the system
  • Avoid running extra water (irrigation, extra laundry loads) until the leak is located, to keep the diagnosis clean
  • Don't attempt to dig or cut into a wall yourself before the leak is precisely located

How we diagnose and fix it

We use acoustic listening equipment to find the sound signature of pressurized water escaping underground or in a wall, and thermal imaging to spot the temperature difference a hot-water leak leaves behind. Both methods locate the leak within a small, confirmed radius before any cutting or digging starts.

Once located, the repair path depends on where the leak sits: a spot repair for an isolated slab or yard leak, or a longer conversation about reroute versus repipe if the same section of line has failed more than once. We'll walk through both honestly rather than defaulting to the more expensive option.

Any repair that follows leak detection, whether a spot repair or a full reroute, has to use approved materials and methods under Georgia's plumbing code (the International Plumbing Code, or IPC, with state amendments). The diagnostic step itself isn't code-regulated, but the fix it leads to always is.

Avoiding the next call

The earliest warning of a hidden leak is often the water bill, not a visible sign. Checking the meter reading before bed and again in the morning with everything off is a simple test any homeowner can run before calling, and it's worth doing if a bill spike shows up with no explanation.

Meter and bill

Water meter running or a bill that jumped

The most common reason people call for leak detection is a number, not a puddle. The meter keeps turning when nothing is running, or the bill climbs well past a normal month. Both point to water escaping somewhere you cannot see.

Turn off everything that uses water, including the ice maker and any irrigation timer, then watch the meter. If it still moves, the leak is real. If the meter stands still with the main valve closed but moves with it open, the loss is on the house side of the meter. Tell dispatch what you saw and we plan the visit around it.

Utility says you have a leak

A notice or alert from the water utility is worth acting on. We find where the water is going so you can decide on a repair.

Bill rose slowly over months

A slow climb often means a small, steady leak such as a toilet, a supply line, or a buried section. Slow does not mean harmless.

House is for sale

A suspected leak during a sale is time sensitive. Tell dispatch so we can schedule around the closing timeline.

Ceilings, walls and yards

Leaks behind walls, under slabs, and in the yard

Water behind a wall or above a ceiling usually announces itself with staining, a soft spot in drywall, or a musty smell. A leak above a kitchen or dining room ceiling often starts in the bathroom above it. A garage wall that stays damp can mean a supply line behind the drywall.

Outside, a soggy patch, greener grass over a line, or water near the meter box points to a buried pipe. We use acoustic and thermal equipment to locate the leak, mark the spot, and explain the repair options before anything is opened. For a leak under a floor or driveway, see our slab leak repair page.

FAQ

Emergency questions about leak detection in Athens

My water meter keeps running even with everything off. What does that mean?

That almost always means an underground or slab leak somewhere between the meter and the house. We can confirm the exact location with leak detection equipment.

Will you have to cut into my floor to find the leak?

No, not to find it. Acoustic and thermal equipment locates the leak first. Cutting only happens at the confirmed repair spot, not as part of the search.

How fast can you get someone out for leak detection?

Same-day in most cases, and it's treated as urgent given the water damage a slab or wall leak can cause the longer it runs.

Does leak detection damage my property?

No, the acoustic and thermal equipment we use is non-invasive. Nothing gets cut or dug until the leak's confirmed location is known.

Is a running water meter an emergency?

If the meter runs with every fixture off, water is being lost somewhere, and it can get worse. Close the main valve, call, and tell dispatch whether the meter stops. If you also see water spreading, treat it as an emergency.

How do you find a leak without opening walls?

We use acoustic and thermal equipment to locate the leak first, so a repair crew is not guessing where to open a wall, a floor, or the yard. You get a marked location and a plain explanation of what repair would involve.

Do you also repair the leak once you find it?

Yes. After we locate the leak we explain the repair options and what each one involves, so you can decide. Some people ask only for detection and repair it themselves, and that is fine too.

My water bill jumped. Is it definitely a leak?

Not always, but a leak is the first thing to rule out. Run the meter test above. A meter that moves with everything off is strong evidence, and we can confirm where the loss is.

Can you find a leak in an irrigation line?

Tell dispatch when you call if the water loss may be in sprinkler or irrigation lines. We can help work out whether the leak is in the house plumbing or somewhere outdoors, and we explain what we can and cannot repair.

Do you handle leak detection for businesses and multi-unit buildings?

Yes. Triplexes, small apartment buildings, and commercial properties are common. Our commercial plumbing page covers leaks in tenant spaces, parking lots, and floor drains.

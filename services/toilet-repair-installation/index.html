Emergency toilet repair: overflow, leaks, and backups

A toilet is an emergency when it is overflowing and will not stop, when the tank or bowl is cracked and leaking, or when a supply line has let go. Turn the shutoff valve behind the toilet clockwise to stop the water, then call.

If the toilet backs up and other drains gurgle or water shows at baseboards, the problem is probably in the main line, not the toilet. Tell dispatch so we send the right equipment.

Call right away if

  • A toilet overflowing and not stopping
  • A cracked tank or bowl leaking onto the floor
  • A supply line or shutoff valve that has failed
  • A toilet backing up along with a tub, shower, or floor drain

A common issue in older Athens homes and rentals

Original toilets in Athens' older in-town homes and University of Georgia rental units are frequently past their realistic service life, and a wax ring or flapper that was fine five tenants ago often isn't anymore. High rental turnover means these fixtures see more use and less consistent maintenance attention than an owner-occupied home, which is a real, practical reason we see more toilet leaks and running-toilet calls in that segment of the housing stock.

A property manager overseeing several rental units in Athens' UGA-adjacent housing market often finds it worth standardizing on one toilet model across units, since it simplifies parts inventory and repair time when the same wax ring or flapper fits every unit in the portfolio.

Toilets in student-heavy neighborhoods

In Five Points and the blocks around South Milledge Avenue, many houses are shared by students. Fixtures in shared houses are used constantly, and a running toilet wastes water around the clock before anyone owns the problem. We coordinate repairs with the property manager so the fix is approved quickly.

Signs to watch for

Signs you need toilet repair & installation

  • Toilet runs continuously or intermittently after flushing
  • Weak or incomplete flush
  • Wobbling at the base or water pooling at the floor
  • Frequent clogging even with normal use
  • A tank lid that doesn't seal properly or a handle that sticks

How we diagnose and fix it

We diagnose whether the issue is the flapper, fill valve, wax ring seal at the base, or a cracked tank or bowl, and repair or replace only what's actually failed rather than recommending a full unit swap for a simple flapper issue.

Most running-toilet and weak-flush issues are inexpensive flapper or fill valve repairs. A wobbly base or water at the floor usually means the wax ring seal has failed and needs replacement. A cracked tank or bowl, or a toilet original to a decades-old home, is when full replacement makes more sense.

Avoiding the next call

A toilet's wax ring and internal components have a normal service life. In high-turnover rental housing, checking them between tenants rather than waiting for a leak to show up on the ceiling below is a cheap way for a landlord to avoid a much larger repair.

Avoiding flushable wipes, which are a common, real cause of clogs despite the label, is a simple habit that prevents a large share of the toilet-clog calls we get. We'll also check the wax ring and flooring underneath during any toilet replacement, since installing a new toilet on a compromised old seal just delays the same failure rather than fixing it.

If the same toilet has needed more than one repair in a short span, that's usually the point where replacement becomes the more economical choice rather than paying for a third visit on the same aging fixture. We stock common flapper, fill valve, and wax ring parts on the truck, which is why most toilet repairs are completed in a single visit rather than requiring a return trip.

A pre-move-out or pre-move-in inspection of every toilet in a rental unit is a small, worthwhile step for landlords managing turnover between tenants.

Symptoms

Running, leaking, and not flushing

Most toilet calls fall into a few patterns. A toilet that runs on its own often needs a new fill valve or flapper. A leak at the base points to a worn wax ring. A weak or broken flush often comes from a failed lever or chain.

If the toilet will not flush and the water does not go down, stop flushing and call. A repeated clog may mean a deeper problem.

Running toilet

A fill valve or flapper that does not seal lets water run into the bowl. It wastes water and is usually a quick repair.

Leak at the base

Water around the base can mean a worn wax ring or a loose bolt. Left alone it can damage the floor.

Broken flush lever

A lever or chain that has failed is a common repair. We also replace worn tank parts.

Two toilets running

Running toilets in more than one bathroom suggest checking the water pressure and the age of the parts. We check both.

Replacement

When to replace instead of repair

A cracked bowl or tank cannot be repaired and should be replaced. An older toilet that keeps needing parts may also be worth replacing. We install new toilets and explain the options before we start.

FAQ

Emergency questions about toilet repair & installation in Athens

Why does my toilet keep running?

Usually a worn flapper or fill valve that isn't sealing properly. It's a quick, inexpensive fix in most cases.

My toilet is wobbly at the base. Is that serious?

It often means the wax ring seal has failed, which can let water leak onto the subfloor if left too long. Worth addressing promptly.

Do you install water-efficient toilets?

Yes, we can install any standard or water-efficient model as part of a replacement.

Can I install a toilet I've already purchased?

Yes, we can install a customer-supplied toilet. We'll let you know if anything about the specific model affects the installation.

What do I do if a toilet is overflowing?

Turn the shutoff valve behind the toilet clockwise, stop flushing, and call. If the valve will not close, shut off the main water valve.

My toilet and tub are both backing up. Is that the toilet?

Probably not. A backup in several fixtures points to the main drain line. Stop using water and call dispatch.

Why does my toilet run on its own?

A worn flapper or fill valve is the usual cause. It wastes water and is often a quick fix, but running toilets in several bathrooms can point to a pressure issue.

Can you replace a cracked toilet?

Yes. A cracked tank or bowl cannot be repaired, so we replace it and explain the options before we start.
